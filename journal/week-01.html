The question

In Haidian District, green spaces and cultural heritage sites are associated with different land-cover patterns, and I could begin to examine this relationship using land-cover data.

The data

I used the Site Scanner to examine Beijing Haidian District. I used Satellite (true colour) as the base layer, and added three layers: Land Cover, When It Was Built, and Summer Surface Heat. The Land Cover layer is from ESA WorldCover (2021) at 10 m resolution. The When It Was Built layer is from GHSL P2023A and shows built-up areas across different periods. The Summer Surface Heat layer uses MODIS data to show daytime land surface temperature during summer. These layers allow me to compare land cover, the built environment, and surface temperature across Haidian. However, the Site Scanner does not provide a specific cultural heritage layer, so cultural heritage sites cannot be directly identified from these data.

I am using Land Cover to describe the spatial distribution of different land-cover types, and it might fail because the categories are broad and do not capture every type of land use. I am using When It Was Built to describe differences in the age and development history of built-up areas, and it might fail because the data uses a relatively coarse 100 m grid. I am using Summer Surface Heat to represent spatial differences in surface temperature, and it might fail because it measures land surface temperature rather than air temperature.

The method

I first viewed the whole of Beijing using Satellite (true colour) as the base layer. I then zoomed in to the whole of Haidian District and examined the three overlay layers: Land Cover, When It Was Built, and Summer Surface Heat. Finally, I zoomed in further to look at individual neighbourhoods, including Wansliu, Qinghe, and Xiangshan. I adjusted the layer opacity to approximately 0.20–0.45 so that the overlay and the satellite imagery could both be seen clearly.

What I found

The Land Cover map shows a clear pattern in the distribution of built-up and vegetated areas across Beijing. Most of the urban area has a high density of built-up land and relatively few blue-green areas. However, some larger blue-green patches remain in the central area. There are also noticeable clusters of greener land around Sijiqing and the Gucheng area to the west. This suggests that green and blue land-cover types are unevenly distributed rather than spread evenly across the city.

What this does not show

This map shows broad land-cover patterns across Beijing, but it does not show the exact boundaries or quality of individual green spaces. It also cannot show whether green areas are accessible to people or how they are used. My observation focuses on the city-scale view shown in the screenshot; if I zoomed in further, smaller patches of green space and differences between neighbourhoods might become more visible.
