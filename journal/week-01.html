The question

In Haidian District, green spaces and cultural heritage sites are associated with different land-use patterns and surface temperatures, and I could examine this relationship using surface temperature data.

The data

I used the Site Scanner to examine Beijing Haidian District. I used Satellite (true colour) as the base layer, and added three layers: Land Cover, When It Was Built, and Summer Surface Heat. The Land Cover layer is from ESA WorldCover (2021) at 10 m resolution. The When It Was Built layer is from GHSL P2023A and shows built-up areas across different periods. The Summer Surface Heat layer uses MODIS data to show daytime land surface temperature during summer. These layers allow me to compare land cover, the built environment, and surface temperature across Haidian. However, the Site Scanner does not provide a specific cultural heritage layer, so cultural heritage sites cannot be directly identified from these data.

I am using Summer Surface Heat to represent spatial differences in surface temperature, and it might fail because it measures land surface temperature rather than air temperature.

The method

I first viewed the whole of Beijing using Satellite (true colour) as the base layer. I then zoomed in to the whole of Haidian District and examined the three overlay layers: Land Cover, When It Was Built, and Summer Surface Heat. Finally, I zoomed in further to look at individual neighbourhoods, including Wansliu, Qinghe, and Xiangshan. I adjusted the layer opacity to approximately 0.20–0.45 so that the overlay and the satellite imagery could both be seen clearly.
