Week 02 - Atmosphere

Climate normals for Beijing

The question

How does Beijing’s seasonal temperature and precipitation distribution compare with Shanghai, Guangzhou, and China’s national pattern?

The data

World Historical Climate — Monthly Averages for GHCN-D Stations for 1981–2010, published by Esri from NOAA NCEI GHCN-Daily v3.24. Opened on September 23, 2026. The averaging window is 1981–2010. The description says 4,620 stations, but the table shows 7,480 rows.

The method

I filtered the layer with GHCN-D ID starts with CH, which returned 226 Chinese stations. I then added AND GHCN-D ID is CHM00054511 to isolate Beijing. I charted the twelve [Month] Avg. Temp C fields and the twelve [Month] Mean Prcp mm fields, using Mean aggregation.

The evidence

What I found

Beijing’s hottest month is July, about 26.7°C, and its coldest is January, about -3.1°C. July is also the wettest month, about 160 mm, while December is the driest, about 2 mm. The rain falls in the hot season, which is the East Asian monsoon pattern. Compared with Shanghai, Beijing is colder and drier; compared with Guangzhou, Beijing has a much stronger winter and a shorter wet season. Within China, Beijing shows a northern monsoon distribution with strong seasonal clustering. Station density is much higher in eastern China than in the west.

What this does not show

The 1981–2010 window is not the current WMO normal (1991–2020). Monthly means hide daily variance. One station is not a place; it is one instrument, often at an airport. Points are not a surface, and empty areas are not average—they are places with no instrument or no digitised record. Web Mercator also distorts area and density at high latitudes. Nothing here is causal.
